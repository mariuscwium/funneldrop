Valuation is ARR × a revenue multiple: 6× at flat growth (a typical private SaaS level, simplified), plus 0.4× for every 1% of monthly MRR growth, between 2× and 25×. Growth is measured over the last 3 months (30 seconds). Users keep their horizontal position as they fall from one board to the next, so slot positions interact. Base slot widths are tuned by chained simulation (10,000 users per board) so landing rates match benchmarks. Upgrade widths are interpolated from smaller test runs, and rates drift by a few points once several boards are upgraded. Acquisition 5%: freemium sign-up without a credit card runs about 2–10% of visitors (Powered by Search). Activation 36%: average across 500+ products in Lenny’s survey (summary). Retention 47%: average month-1 retention across 83 B2B SaaS companies (Userpilot 2024). Referral 10%: typical for early-stage SaaS; mature companies reach 25–30% (Alexander Jarvis). Each referral brings 1 to 25 friends, usually around 8, who arrive already signed up. Paying customers can also refer: 1% a month each, counting up to 1,000 customers. Referred friends don’t refer again. Both are game simplifications, not measured data, as are team effects, random events and how fast content goes stale. Revenue 30%: derived so that about 5% of all sign-ups end up paying, matching freemium’s 3–7% (Single Grain). Paid churn 5% a month: the top of the 2.5–5% range Lenny calls good for SMB and mid-market B2B (Lenny’s Newsletter). Order follows Dave McClure’s original 2007 AARRR talk, with Referral before Revenue: every retained user can refer, and then some of them pay. Many later infographics swap the last two. Price: benchmarks apply at $49. The effect of other prices on conversion and churn is a game simplification, not measured data.
