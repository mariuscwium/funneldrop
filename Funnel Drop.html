Each ad sends visitors. Every ad on a channel reaches 8% fewer people than the last; reach recovers slowly over time, or a new campaign resets it. They fall through the five pirate metrics in order: Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue. Paying customers pay $49 a month to start, until they cancel (6% a month), and about 22% of that goes on hosting and support. You can change the price. A month passes every 10 seconds.

Valuation is ARR × a revenue multiple: 6× at flat growth (a typical private SaaS level, simplified), plus 0.4× for every 1% of monthly MRR growth, between 2× and 25×. Growth is measured over the last 3 months (30 seconds). Users keep their horizontal position as they fall from one board to the next, so slot positions interact. Base slot widths are tuned by chained simulation (10,000 users per board) so landing rates match benchmarks, except Acquisition. Upgrade widths are interpolated from smaller test runs, and rates drift by a few points once several boards are upgraded. Acquisition 5%: freemium sign-up without a credit card runs about 2–10% of visitors (Powered by Search). The game starts nearer 7%, inside that range. Activation 36%: average across 500+ products in Lenny’s survey (summary). Retention 47%: average month-1 retention across 83 B2B SaaS companies (Userpilot 2024). Referral 10%: typical for early-stage SaaS; mature companies reach 25–30% (Alexander Jarvis). Each referral brings 1 to 25 friends, usually around 8, who arrive already signed up. Paying customers can also refer: 1% a month each, counting up to 1,000 customers. Referred friends don’t refer again. Both are game simplifications, not measured data, as are team effects, marketing managers’ diminishing returns, new hires’ six-month ramp-up, yearly raises and departures, channel differences, random events, how fast content goes stale and how much search traffic swings from month to month (up to 40% either way). Above a $100k valuation, ads and campaigns cost more and reach more as the company grows, with price rising faster, so each visitor costs more at scale. How fast is a game simplification. Revenue 30%: derived so that about 5% of all sign-ups end up paying, matching freemium’s 3–7% (Single Grain). Paid churn 6% a month: a tough start for a low-priced product, above the 2.5–5% range Lenny calls good for SMB and mid-market B2B (Lenny’s Newsletter). Upgrades bring it to 4% at best. Published SMB figures vary widely by source. Order follows Dave McClure’s original 2007 AARRR talk, with Referral before Revenue: every retained user can refer, and then some of them pay. Many later infographics swap the last two. Cost to serve 22%: hosting and support, so gross margin is 78%. That is close to the 77% median subscription margin in KeyBanc’s 2022 private SaaS survey (Kellblog summary). The 22% itself is a game simplification. Lifetime value and payback use gross margin. Net revenue retention is a chained monthly approximation: each month, (starting MRR + upgrades − downgrades − churned MRR) ÷ starting MRR, multiplied over the last 12 months. New customers’ churn is mostly left out. Median net dollar retention was 109% in the same KeyBanc survey, and Clouded Judgement’s cloud software comps showed a median net retention of 112% in January 2024 (source). Price: benchmarks apply at $49. The effect of other prices on conversion and churn is a game simplification, not measured data. Expansion: from $1M valuation, each paying customer is checked about once a year and can move up or down one plan (Base, Pro, Enterprise). How often, the 12% upgrade and 4% downgrade rates, and the plan prices are game simplifications, not measured data. IPO readiness: ARR of $100M, net revenue retention of 110%, a Rule of 40 score of 40 (year-on-year ARR growth % plus profit margin %, a rule of thumb from Brad Feld) and 30% year-on-year growth, all held for 3 months in a row. In the late 2010s, “IPO scale” was commonly put at $100M ARR growing 30% a year; the bar is higher now (Scale Venture Partners). The 110% retention target and the 3-month hold are game simplifications, and none of these are listing requirements. Profit margin is revenue minus all spending, over revenue, for the last 3 months.
