# CLAUDE.md

Funnel Drop: a browser game that teaches SaaS funnel economics. Visitors fall through five Plinko-style boards, one per pirate metric (Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue). The player spends cash on ads, upgrades, staff and loans to grow MRR and valuation.

## Working here

Everything is in `Funnel Drop.html`: inline CSS, markup and JS. There is no build or package manager. Open the file in a browser to run it. The name contains a space, so quote it in shell commands.

Run tests with `node --test 'tests/*.test.js'` (Node 22, no packages). `tests/harness.js` runs the game headless; `el('ev').open`, `el('sum').open` and `el('over').open` must stay false or `loop` pauses. `load({seed})` installs a seeded `Math.random` in the game for statistical tests. Perf tests time CPU (`process.cpuUsage`), best of 5 after a warm-up, because the test files run in parallel.

Fonts load from Google Fonts. There are no other runtime dependencies. Progress saves to `localStorage`, so reloading resumes the game; the New game button wipes it.

## Simulator

`sim/` holds the original tuning scripts, kept as they were run. Both are unseeded and duplicate the game's physics rather than sharing it. `sweep.js` loads the first 9 lines of `sim.js`, so keep the physics there.

```sh
# Chained rates for all five boards, 10,000 users each. Takes about 35s.
node sim/sim.js '[[[168,192]],[[51,131],[229,309]],[[42,122],[238,318]],[[156,204]],[[122,238]]]'

# Landing rate per slot width for one board (0-4), upstream boards at base widths.
node sim/sweep.js 0 23 28 31 34

# Expansion board: Upgrade and Downgrade rates for Downgrade width 9 (12px from the walls) and centre widths 43 and 56.
G=12 node sim/expansion.js 9 43 56
```

`sweep.js` still chains upstream boards from the old base widths (Acquisition 16, Activation 72), so its results for boards 1-4 are slightly off.

The sims differ from the game: fixed 1/180s timestep, no 600 speed cap, a 22s timeout instead of 12s, and no referred friends.

## Code map

The script is one top-level block, in this order:

- Constants, `BOARDS` (six boards: the five pirate metrics, then Expansion; slot zones, benchmarks) and peg layout, synthesised sound (`SFX`, `sfx`)
- Channels and organic traffic: `CHANNELS`, scale pricing (`mkt`, `adCost`, `campCost`, `perAd`), `SEO`, `mmEff`, `buyAd`, `post`
- Simulation: `enter`, `land` (Expansion in `landExp`), `expCheck`, `physics`
- Rendering: `draw` (canvas), `hud` (DOM), `CHARTS` (SVG lines fed by `hist`, a point every 10 game days (`HIST_PER` 3 a month), last `HIST_N` (73) points = 24 months on a fixed axis; saves mark this with `histDays:10`), `STAGES` (the pirate metrics funnel)
- `loop`: the `requestAnimationFrame` tick. Handles cash, reach recovery, payroll, loans, churn, and at each month rollover the `ledger` entry, `expCheck`, the IPO hold (`ipoHold`) and the game-over checks (`rollRisk`)
- Roadmap nodes (`NODES`, `linkNodes`, `refx`, `nodeState`, `buyNode`, `respec`; effects summed into `FX`, defined near the top), the roadmap modal (`nodePos`, `renderTree`, `renderDetail`, `openTree`, `treeBuy`, pan and zoom input), team (`CSM`, `MM`, `ROLES`, staff cache `restaff`, bulk `hireN`), financing (`LOANS`), random events (`EVENTS`), milestones (`MILES`), game-overs (`atRisk`, `warn`, `rollRisk`, cases `ruleCase`, the board `boardMonth`, the bridge `takeBridge`, `endGame`, `openOver`), IPO readiness and the final summary (`IPO`, `ipoChecks`, `ipoHud`, `fileIPO`, `openSum`), the alignment reveal on both end screens (`revealHtml`), responsive layout (`placeBuy`), persistence (`save`, `load`)

## Gotchas

- **Slot widths are tuned, not derived.** Base widths in `BOARDS` were checked with `sim/sim.js`. `RATES[b].w` holds `[width, rate]` points per board (ascending). The first four points are the base width and the tuned widths of the old upgrade tiers; points beyond come from `sim/sweep.js` / `sim/expansion.js`, each curve scaled through the fourth point. `RATES[b]` also holds `id`, `label`, `unit` and `base` (the first point's rate). `widthFor(b, r)` interpolates piecewise-linearly and clamps to the table ends; `rateAt(b, x)` is its inverse over width (in proportion below the first point, the last segment extended past the end); `rateCap(b)` is the last rate; `bonus[b]` is the rate added by roadmap nodes (`FX['r'+b]`, set by `refx`). `cur(t)` is `base + bonus` up to `RATE_KNEE` (70%) of the headroom `rateCap - base`, then closes in on `rateCap` exponentially (same slope at the knee), so every rate node still adds something late; negative bonuses count as 0. Tests may set `bonus[b]` and call `zone(b)` directly; the next `refx` overwrites it. Changing pegs, gravity, ball size or `CENTERS` invalidates the widths and the benchmark claims. Acquisition is deliberately above its 5% benchmark: 24px lands about 7.3%, and Activation is 80px to hold 36% with that wider slot above it.
- **Users keep their x position between boards**, so slot positions on one board affect rates on the next. Every `RATES` point beyond the base assumes base widths upstream, so real rates drift from the table when several boards are widened by nodes or staff: with the old tier-3 widths on every board (the tables' fourth points), Retention landed near 54% against 56.6%.
- **Funnel flow is not strictly linear.** All retained users pass through Referral to Revenue, whether or not they refer. A referral adds 1 to 25 friends (`referred`, peaking near 8) directly to Activation. Referred friends never refer again, though they still land in the slot and count in `out[3]`. Paying customers also refer at a flat 1% a month, counting at most `REFER_CAP` customers; those referrals skip `out[3]`. Both limits stop referrals compounding, so keep them when rebalancing.
- **`CENTERS` must match the zone midpoints in `BOARDS`.** `zone` rebuilds zones from `CENTERS`.
- **Colours are tokens.** `:root` holds the light theme and `:root[data-theme=dark]` overrides it. A small script before the stylesheet sets `data-theme` from the saved `funnel-drop-theme` choice, or the system setting if there isn't one. Canvas colours come from `C`, so add new ones to `readColors`, which reruns on every theme change. Text on `--ad` uses `--on-ad`, which doesn't change with the theme.
- **Numbers are repeated in copy.** The intro in `#msg` (phone only; desktop keeps the space blank until the first message) and the sources paragraph in `.src` hard-code values such as $49, 8%, 7% and 10 seconds. Update them when the constants change, and keep the cited sources accurate.
- **New game state must be added to `save` and `load`.** Zones, `bonus` and `churn` are rebuilt from `owned` by `refx`, not saved. `owned`, `paid` (points paid per node id, added to by `buyNode`), `rp`, `stale`, `respecUsed` and `alHist` are saved. Old saves have `lvl` and no `owned`: `load` owns the seed node of each track at tier 1+ and refunds 2 points for tier 2 and 3 for tier 3 on top of `RP_START`, plus the `RP_MILE` points of every milestone the save's `peak` has passed (old saves never saw them, and `msLvl=milesHit()` on load means they would never be granted). The new save has `owned`, so it never converts twice. `respec()` is one per run (`respecUsed`): it needs something owned besides `start`, `respecCost()` (max of $5,000 and 3x MRR) in cash and no `ipoFiled`, otherwise it returns false and keeps the respec; refunds all `paid` into `rp` (may pass the cap), resets `owned` to the start node (which frees fork locks) and zeroes `stale`. Bump the `SAVE` key if the saved shape changes.
- **Price effects are multipliers that equal 1 at `PRICE0` ($49).** `priceConv` (S-curve) scales the Revenue slot width, which works because landing rate is close to linear in width there (`sim/sweep.js 4`, widths 8 to 320, slightly above the line below 26). `priceChurn` (U-shape) scales paid churn. Their curves are invented, so keep them out of the benchmark claims.
- **Marketing managers have diminishing returns.** Visitors, the SEO boost, slower staleness and post reach all use `mmEff()` = `dimEff(MM_K, MM_P, strength('mm'))`, where `dimEff(K,P,m)` = `K·((1+m/K)^P−1)/P` is about 1 per full hire at first and grows like `m^P`. Never use `strength('mm')` for an effect directly. The Marketing manager and Engineer cards compute their "10 do the work of about N" copy from `dimEff`, so retuning keeps it right.
- **Ads get dearer at scale.** `mkt()` is peak valuation over `MKT0` ($100k), at least 1. `adCost(c)` and `campCost(c)` scale the base prices by `mkt()^AD_PRICE_P`; visitors per ad are `perAd(c)` = `c.reach × mkt()^AD_REACH_P`. `c.reach` stays in base units (the reach bar, decay and recovery are unchanged). Never read `c.cost`, `c.camp` or `c.reach` as a price or visitor count. Below $100k everything is identical to before.
- **SEO traffic decays.** `seoFresh` falls 15% a month to a floor of 10%, slower with marketing managers. Publishing resets it for 15% of the current tier's price, and that spend counts toward CAC. Search traffic also swings: each month rollover sets `seoRoll` (saved) to a uniform multiplier within ±`SEO_VAR` (40%). `seoBase()` is the average and `seoRange()` the range the SEO card and messages show; `organicRate()` applies the roll to search traffic only, not marketing managers' own visitors.
- **Milestones (`MILES`) key off `peak` valuation**, so `msLvl` is rebuilt on load and old saves don't replay them. They share the `#ev` dialog through `showEv`. `mileMonth` records the game month each one is first reached; saves from before it leave those empty, shown as "–". An optional 4th entry replaces the "Next up" text (Unicorn points at the IPO).
- **The IPO is the win.** It and the endings below are mutually exclusive: `atRisk()` is false once either `ipoFiled` or `over` is set, so a filed company can't lose and a lost one can't file. `ipoChecks()` lists ARR, NRR (`NRR12`), Rule of 40 (`yoy()` + `margin3()`, in points) and year-on-year growth (`yoy()`: MRR now against `ledger.at(-13)`); a null value is never ok and shows "–". `ipoHold` counts month rollovers in a row with all four ok and resets on any miss; File for IPO shows at `IPO.hold`. `fileIPO` sets `ipoFiled` and snapshots the summary rows into `ipoSnap`, so it doesn't drift while the boards keep running. While `ipoFiled`, every buy (including `buyNode`), hire, borrow, unlock, publish, post, campaign and price handler returns early and its button is disabled; letting staff go and paying off loans still work. Random events, annual reviews and milestones stop too. New spending actions must check `ipoFiled`. `#sum` pauses `loop` like `#ev` and shares its close handler; reloading a filed game reopens it. `playMs` sums frame time while running and stops at filing.
- **Game-overs.** Three endings, `ENDINGS`: `runway` (Ran out of runway), `board` (Ousted by the board) and `shut` (Shut down). `atRisk()` (`!ipoFiled&&!over`) is the one guard: every spending handler, `rollRisk`, the annual review, random events and milestones check it. `rollRisk()` runs at each month rollover after the IPO hold, in this order: insolvency, the soft-lock, the board, then a case ruling. Annual review or a random event follow only if still `atRisk()`. Every ending goes through `endGame(kind,title,text)` and needs an earlier `warn(cause,...)` for the same cause: `warn` logs `{cause,month,text}` to `warnLog` (last 20, saved) and opens `#ev` through `showEv`, which queues in `evQueue` when `#ev` is already open, so an event and a warning in one rollover show one after the other. `endGame` clears that queue, sets `over={kind,month,story,snap}` (`story` is the last 3 warnings for the cause plus the ending, `snap` the `ipoSummary('At the end')` rows), saves and opens `#over`, which pauses `loop`; `#over-reveal` is left empty. Reloading a lost game reopens it. `#over` can't be dismissed (its `cancel` is prevented), a lost game can't file for the IPO, and once `over` is set `loop` skips the rest of that frame (payroll, loans, churn) so the state matches `snap`.
  - *Insolvency:* `insolventNow()` is cash below 0 or a late loan, counted only from `RISK_AT` ($100k, the First traction milestone) peak valuation and only after a first paying customer (`out[4]`) or with a loan held. Below `RISK_AT` an overdrawn company just stays overdrawn and the bridge isn't offered, so the early game's slow starts (often 0-5 customers for years) aren't ended by a tax bill. Each insolvent rollover adds to `insolvent`, a solvent one resets it; the first warns, a second warning comes at `INSOLVENT_MONTHS`−1, and the `INSOLVENT_MONTHS`th (6) ends the run. The Company panel shows a "Can’t pay bills: closes in N months" row (`cInsol`) while `insolvent>0`. `runway()` is cash over monthly burn from `netFlow()` (null when flow isn't negative); the Company panel shows it.
  - *Soft-lock:* `softLocked()` is cash below the first channel's ad, no balls or queue, no paying customers, at least one ad bought, no organic traffic and no free post possible (`!canPost()`). It warns on the first month and ends at `SOFTLOCK_MONTHS` (2). A post can bring visitors whenever the run is on, cooling down or not, so `canPost()` is just `atRisk()` and the soft-lock can't fire in the current game; it only matters if something later takes posting away (tests stub `canPost`). Before `canPost`, runs sat in this state for up to 30 months and then posted their way out, some to the IPO.
  - *Lifeline:* the first insolvency warning carries a Take it button (`showEv`'s `act`) while `bridgeOffer()`: once a run (`bridgeUsed`). `takeBridge()` fills the hidden `LOANS` entry `bridge` with `bridgeSize()`: the overdraft (`-cash`) plus `BRIDGE_MONTHS` (3) of burn, rounded up to $500, at least `BRIDGE_MIN` ($5,000) × `mkt()^AD_PRICE_P` (rounded down), repaid over 12 months at `BRIDGE_APR` (45%). With users exposure at .3 or above it is Customer prepayments instead, same size at 0%. Both queue a −15 board hit (×1.5 at process exposure −.3 or below, like every negative hit).
  - *The board* exists from the $1M milestone (`checkMilestone` creates it with `freshBoard()`; `load` creates one at `BOARD_START` (70) for an old save already past $1M, with no ultimatum). `boardMonth()` adds up `boardReasons()` (Nothing shipped −1 per `stale` month past `STALE_GRACE`, up to −3; Growing +2 above 20% `yoy()`, or 3-month growth annualised before a year of ledger; Shrinking −2 below 0; Runway under 6 months −2; Can’t pay bills −5), the one-off hits queued by `boardHit` in `pend` (scandal events −6, Investigation and Audit −10, Consent order and Audit failed −20, Case closed +5, emergency funding −15, each later milestone +10; negative hits ×1.5 at process exposure −.3 or below) and Shipped +3 for a month with a node bought. With no reason it drifts 1 toward `BOARD_REST` (60). Below `BOARD_WARN` (40) it warns once (re-armed at 50); below `BOARD_ULT` (20) it sets `board.ult`, a deadline `ultMonths()` away: `ULT_MONTHS` 6, `ULT_LAWFUL` 9 at process exposure .3 or above, `ULT_SHORT` 4 at −.3 or below or within `ULT_REPEAT` (24) months of the last ultimatum. Back above 40 clears it; still below at the deadline ends the run. The sizes are named constants (`BOARD_GROW`, `BOARD_STALE`, `BOARD_UP`, `BOARD_DOWN`, `BOARD_RUNWAY`, `BOARD_RUNWAY_HIT`, `BOARD_BROKE`, `BOARD_SHIP`, `BOARD_DRIFT`, `BOARD_REARM`). The Company row shows the score with a bar (warning colour below 40), the last month's reasons once and the ultimatum countdown; `boardFix(label)` gives each hit's fix for the warning text.
  - *Cases:* the warn events Investigation opened (users) and Security audit ordered (process) need exposure on their axis at `CASE_AT` (−.4) or below, a matching earlier event this run (`evSeen` counts events shown by name, saved) and no open case. `lawCase={axis,due}` rules `CASE_MONTHS` (12) later in `ruleCase()`, on `companyAl()` at ruling time, so buying nodes or a respec in between changes it. `caseBand(x)`: above −.4 closed, above `CASE_SHUT` (−.7) a consent order (`CASE_FINE` (3) × MRR fine, 10-20% of customers leave via `loseCustomers`) or failed audit (`CASE_FINE` × MRR, 30% of Enterprise customers via `removeSubs`), otherwise Shut down. Shut down needs an earlier order on the same axis: `lawStrikes[axis]` counts order rulings (saved), and a shut-band ruling with no strike is ruled an order instead.
  - *Saved:* `over`, `warnLog`, `insolvent`, `softMonths`, `bridgeUsed`, `lawCase`, `lawStrikes`, `evSeen` and `board`, with defaults when missing (`SAVE` isn't bumped). The bridge loan saves with `LOANS`; only the bridge entry keeps its `apr` and `name`. All sizes are invented (see `.src`).
- **All cash out goes through `spendCash(n)`**, which also adds to `monthOut`. Cost to serve is `cts()` (`COST_TO_SERVE`, 22%, times `1+FX.cts`) of `MRR()` each frame, and it just overdraws. Revenue added to cash also goes to `monthRev`; borrowing and grants are neither revenue nor spending. At each month rollover `ledger` gets `{rev,out,mrr}` (last 13 kept), and `margin3()` is the 3-month profit margin. LTV, payback, net cash flow and `health()` use the gross margin `1-cts()` (78% with no nodes). `tests/bot.js early` is a minimal greedy early-game check; see Balance for the full bot.
- **Cash can go negative**, from a tax bill or a case fine. A negative balance at a rollover counts toward insolvency. Payroll and loan shortfalls must keep any overdraft, not reset cash to 0.
- **Team and financing cards unlock by peak valuation** (`at` on each entry, checked by `gate` in `hud`). Held staff and loans stay visible below it, and these sit on top of each loan's MRR rule.
- **Staff are individuals.** `staff` holds `{role, hired, pay, skill}`, with `hired` in fractional game months (`gameMonths()`). A role's effect is `strength(role)`, the sum of skill × `ramp(e)`, where a new hire starts at `RAMP_START` (25%) and reaches full effect after `RAMP_MONTHS` (6). Use `hire`, `fire` (removes the newest of a role), `heads` and `strength`, never raw counts; ROLES code must stay generic over roles. Zones for boards with ramping staff are rebuilt once a game month (`refitRamping`) and on hire, fire and review. Payroll bounces drop the newest person overall. **`heads`, `strength`, `rampingUp` and `teamCost` read a cache** (`team`, `payroll`), because every odds-path landing reads `strength` through `rateOf`. `hire` adds to it; `restaff()` rebuilds it in `fire`, `annualReview`, `load` and `refitRamping` (each month rollover), so strength steps monthly rather than continuously. Anything else that edits `staff` must call `restaff()`. Bulk hiring (`hireN(role,n)`, the +10 and Cover all buttons, `csmGap()`) needs `n` months of salary in cash and calls `hire` per person. Old saves (`roles`, `csm`, `mm`, `tenure`) convert in `oldStaff`, fully ramped with their years kept.
- **Annual review fires every 12 game months** (`annualReview`) instead of an event. It is per person: anyone with at least 12 months served gets +5% pay and +0.03 skill, and each person's headhunting chance is 5% + 5% per full year served, capped at 40%. New hires cost the base salary.
- **Hidden alignment steers events.** `companyAl()` is summed exposure, not a mean: per axis, Σ `al` × count over owned nodes except `start`, divided by `ALIGN_SCALE` (5) and clamped to -1..1 (`[0,0]` if none), so building outward through near-neutral plain nodes doesn't dilute what the fork choices said. Each month rollover pushes it, rounded to 2 places, to `alHist` (saved). `pickEvent(good)` picks within the good or bad side, weighting an event with `al` by `max(.2, 1 + EVENT_AL_K*(g*eg + l*el))` (`EVENT_AL_K` 2), others 1; `randomEvent` runs it. The alignment bad events (Regulator fine, Class action, Data breach, Viral complaint) have a `when` needing exposure on their axis (`companyAl()` ≤ -.15), so they never fire for a neutral company. Nothing in the UI, node text or event text may name alignment, ethics or the like; `tests/alignment.test.js` checks it. Sizes are invented (see `.src`).
- **The reveal is the only place alignment shows.** `revealHtml()` fills `#sum-reveal` (in `openSum`) and `#over-reveal` (in `openOver`), above the stats. It plots `alHist` as a path from the centre (users `al[0]` up, process `al[1]` right, `REVEAL_R` 85 in a 200 viewBox), a dot every 12 months and "Now" at the end; an empty `alHist` plots `companyAl()`. `revealArch(u,p)` names one of 9 archetypes (`REVEAL_ARCH`) from bands at `REVEAL_BAND` (.15, the same as the exposure-gated events). `revealMoments()` lists up to 3 owned nodes by (|al[0]|+|al[1]|)×count, and `revealLed()` the `al` events in `evSeen`, most often first. It is built from live state when the dialog opens. Its words stay plain business terms too: `tests/reveal.test.js` runs the vocabulary check over every archetype.
- **Events roll once a game month** at `EVENT_CHANCE`, with good news from 10% to 70% depending on `health()`, shifted by `FX.evGood`, and only after the first ad or customer. Each is one-off, not a timed effect, and `when` hides it if it can't apply. Their sizes are invented. An event opens the `#ev` dialog, which pauses `loop`; any new `performance.now()` timer must shift in its `close` handler, shared by `#ev`, `#sum` and `#tree`.
- **Channels differ by `aim`.** Search and LinkedIn visitors enter their aimed board pulled toward its slot by `pull`, and `lift` is the matching rate for the odds path. Pulling only works where incoming x is spread out: Acquisition and Revenue doubled or so, while Activation and Retention barely moved. `queue` holds `{t,src}`.
- **At most `ballCap` users animate.** `spawn` lands the rest by the odds, so always add users through it. The cap adapts to the device (`adaptCap`, called from `loop` with each frame's interval): from `BALL_MIN` (400) up to `BALL_MAX` (6000), shrinking a fifth when 30 focused frames average over 18 ms and growing a tenth while they keep up and the cap is full, under a ceiling `capCeil` that remembers where frames dropped. Frames over 100 ms or while the window lacks focus don't count, since Orca throttles unfocused windows to about 20 fps. Outcomes don't depend on the cap. Saved balls go back through `spawn` on load, so a save with more balls than the starting cap lands the extra ones by the odds.
- **The roadmap (tech tree) replaces the Upgrades list.** `NODES` entries are `{id, name, desc, area, ring, slot, links, fork, pts, cash, al, fx, rep}`: `area` 0-5 is a funnel wedge and `[a,b]` a keystone between two; `ring` is the distance out (the `start` node is ring 0, always owned, free); `slot` is the angle in the wedge (-1..1); `al` is the hidden alignment `[g,l]`, each -1..1, never shown. `linkNodes()` builds two-way `adj` and the id index (`nodeOf(id)`); rerun it after changing `NODES`. `nodeState(id)` is `owned`, `available` (linked to an owned node; an owned repeatable stays available), `unreachable`, `locked` (another node with the same `fork` id is owned) or `closed` (Expansion wedge before `expOpen()`). `nodeCash(n)` scales `n.cash` by `mkt()^AD_PRICE_P` like ads; `nodePts(n)` is `pts` plus one per repeat. `buyNode(id)` needs `available`, enough `rp` and cash, and `!ipoFiled`, and changes nothing otherwise. `owned` (id → count) feeds `refx()`, which sums each node's `fx` times its count into `FX` and sets `bonus`, `churn` and every zone; call it after editing `owned`. **Hot paths read `FX`, never `owned`.** Effect keys (all deltas): `r0`..`r5` rate points on a board; `evGood` points on the good-news chance; relative `churn`, `adCost` (ads and campaigns), `adReach` (`perAd`), `refSize` (`referred`), `custRef` (`custRefer()`), `cts` (`cts()`), `csm` (`csmCovers()`), `seo` (`seoBase`), `rp` (`rpIncome()`); `priceTol` shrinks how far `priceConv`/`priceChurn` move from 1. Relative effects (`FX_MULT`) multiply: `1+FX.k` is the product of each owned node's `1+v`, so "paid churn −8%" always takes 8% off what is left and a late cut still counts; `refx` floors each at `FX_FLOOR` (`1+FX.k` ≥ .1). `priceTol` adds and is clamped to 0..0.8. Read them only after `refx`. `rp` starts at `RP_START` (2); see Roadmap points. The ring 1 seeds (`acq1`, `act1`, `ret1`, `churn1` in the Retention wedge, `ref1`, `rev1`, `exp1`) carry the old tier 1 upgrades at 1 point and $2,000 ($5,000 for `exp1`, `EXP_CASH` times a seed, which opens at $1M). `#upglist` holds the Roadmap entry card (`#rm-open`), showing points (`#rm-pts`) and, in `#rm-meta`, how many nodes you can buy now (`readyNow()`, recounted at most every 250 ms because `hud` runs every frame).
- **Tree content.** 93 nodes: `start`, 14 nodes per wedge (16 in Retention, which has two seeds) on rings 1-6, and 6 keystones. Every node past the seeds is made by `nd(...)`, which sets cost from the ring: points = ring (keystones 6, repeatables from 4), cash `NODE_CASH[ring]` ([2k, 4k, 8k, 14k, 20k, 25k] for rings 1-6, before `mkt()` scaling), times `EXP_CASH` (2.5) for every node touching Expansion, keystones included. The ladder is deliberately flat at the top: steep deep rings made breadth-first buying dominate and stalled builds around $1-3M, where `mkt()^0.6` prices outran revenue. Each wedge has 3 forks at rings 2-4 (2-3 options, options differ in effect keys and by ≥.5 on one `al` axis, so each fork pairs a plainly useful option with one that pays more and treats users or process worse, or trades one lever for another), plain nodes in between, two ring-5 nodes, and one repeatable at ring 6. Keystones sit on ring 5 at each wedge boundary, link the ring-5 nodes on both sides and lift both boards. Cross-wedge levers are deliberate: `refSize` comes from Activation and Expansion as well as Referral, `custRef` from Retention, `csm` from Retention and the Enterprise keystone, `cts` and `priceTol` from Revenue and Expansion. `tests/tree-content.test.js` pins the structure: per-board reachable rate bonus covers `rateCap(b) - base` while no node past ring 1 adds more than a quarter of it (`act1`'s +9.5 is over that and is kept for old saves), every desc names each effect's number (`+2 points`, `−8%`), all 9 alignment cells (each axis split at ±.33) hold 4+ nodes, no two node centres sit closer than twice the larger radius, and names stay at most 26 characters for the labels. Fork options carry the company's character: plain (non-fork) nodes, seeds and keystones keep `|al|` ≤ .2 and repeatables `[0,0]`, balanced so a build of only plain nodes stays within ±.15 on both axes, while a full build taking the lowest (highest) option on an axis at every fork reaches ≤ −.15 (≥ +.15); tests pin both, plus a shallow growth-hacker path crossing the bad-event gate. Keystones are deliberate back doors: links are two-way, so owning a keystone opens the ring-5 node on the far side, Path of Exile style. Rate bonuses never go negative: below base, `widthFor` returns the base width, so a negative `r` effect does nothing.
- **Roadmap points.** `rpIncome()` a game month = `(RP_FOUNDER + RP_ENG*engEff() + RP_PD*(strength('pd')+strength('pe'))) * (1+FX.rp)`. `engEff()` is shaped like `mmEff()` (`ENG_K`, `ENG_P`) over `strength('eng')`. Each running frame `loop` sets `rp = max(rp, min(rpCap(), rp + income*dt/MONTH))`, so it never lowers an `rp` above `rpCap()` (3 months of income, at least `RP_CAP_MIN`, 6, so founders alone can still save up for a keystone); refunds and milestone bonuses (`RP_MILE`, by `MILES` index, added in `checkMilestone` and named in its dialog) may exceed the cap. Engineers (`'eng'`, `ENG`, unlocks at `ENG.at` peak) are staff like CSMs and MMs but not in `ROLES`; `baseSalary` and `roleName` know them. **Falling behind:** at each month rollover `staleOn` is set to `!ipoFiled && shippable()` (some `available` node affordable in points and cash), and `stale` rises by 1 when it is true; `markShipped()` (called by `buyNode`) and `respec` zero it. A rollover that finds nothing shippable leaves `stale` as it is (no reset), so `stale` counts shippable months since the last ship, not necessarily in a row. `staleMult()` = 1 + `STALE_STEP` per month beyond `STALE_GRACE`, up to `STALE_MAX`, but only while `staleOn` and not `ipoFiled`; otherwise 1. It multiplies `paidChurn()`, which is why `staleOn` is cached rather than calling `shippable()` in churn code. `load` sets `staleOn` the same way. The roadmap card's "Ship something" line shows only while the multiplier is above 1. The roadmap card and the modal header show points, income and cap; the Company panel row "Falling behind competitors" appears while the multiplier is above 1.
- **The roadmap modal (`#tree`) pauses the game** like `#ev`: `loop` only draws while it's open, so no roadmap points or months pass, and its `close` handler shifts `queue`, `postReady` and `nextCut`. `openTree()` sets `pausedAt`, snapshots rates into `TREE.was` and fits the view to every node that isn't `unreachable` or `closed`. **Layout is pure:** `nodePos(n)` puts wedge `a` at -90° + a·60° (Acquisition at the top, clockwise in funnel order), ring `r` at `r*RING_R` (70), and spreads `slot` ±`WEDGE_SPREAD` (25°) inside the wedge, so give seeds sharing a wedge different slots; a keystone `[a,b]` sits on the boundary between its wedges. The view is `TREE.{x,y,k}` (screen = tree·k + x,y; `k` 0.5–3, `TREE_K`), applied as one transform on `#tr-view`, so panning never rebuilds the SVG. `renderTree()` rebuilds everything (wedges, rings, edges, nodes, header, detail) and runs on open, buy and respec; it handles about 100 nodes. Taps select by nearest node within 22 screen px (`hitNode`), not by SVG hit testing, so nodes stay tappable when zoomed out. Node classes follow `nodeState` (`own`, `available`, `locked`, `unreachable`; `can` adds the pulse when affordable; `closed` nodes and their edges aren't drawn and the Expansion wedge shows "Unlocks at $1M"). Node labels are placed by `labelsAt(k)` (via `showLabels`, rerun when the zoom changes, on render and on select): from `LBL_K` up, greedily in screen space so no label overlaps another label or node, selected first, then shipped, then available, then the rest, inner rings first; shown ones get `.lbl` on their `.tr-node`. Box sizes are estimated from the name length (`LBL_CH`), so it runs headless. SVG text is sized `calc(Npx / var(--k))` so it stays the same on screen. Colours are CSS tokens through `--c`/`--c2`, so both themes work without `readColors`. The respec confirm step lives in the detail panel (`TREE.confirm`). The detail panel is a side column from 860px and a bottom sheet below; `showNode` pans a selected or focused node out from under the sheet. Never show `al` in the modal.
- **Physics is the hot path.** `physics` tests a ball only against peg rows (`PEG_ROWS`, per board, in `pegs` order) within `BR+PR` vertically and pegs within that horizontally, which gives exactly the results of testing every peg; `tests/perf.test.js` pins a seeded fingerprint of ball paths, because slot widths are tuned against them. `draw` fills all balls of one colour as one `Path2D`: one fill per ball is what drops frames past a few thousand balls. Measured in Orca on the dev desktop (November 2026): 8,000 balls at a steady 60 fps, 10,000 dropping frames; physics about 0.2 µs and drawing about 0.35 µs per ball per frame.
- **`zone(b)` is the only place that sets a board's slot width.** It uses `slotW(b)`: the node rate (`widthFor(b, cur(RATES[b]))`), times `ROLES` staff (+15% per unit of `strength`) and price, clamped by `maxW(b)` (cached per board) so slots never touch walls, neighbours or the Downgrade slots. `rateOf(t)` is the rate that width actually lands: `rateAt(b, slotW(b))`, capped at `RATE_MAX` (.95). Cards, `p-meta`, the bot, `spawn`'s odds path (aimed `lift` included, also capped) and `expCheck` all read it, so odds-path users land like animated balls whatever `ballCap` is. At base team and $49 it equals `cur(t)`; past a table's end it follows the last measured segment, which is an extrapolation, not sim data. Physics uses the new width at once; `shown(B)` springs the drawn slot over `SPRING` ms. `buyNode` also calls `celebrate` for each board whose rate changed (glow, sparks, floating gain and chime), which hires and price changes skip. While the roadmap is open (`TREE.was` set) it doesn't: closing `#tree` compares rates and paid churn with `TREE.was` and celebrates each changed board once, with one chime (`celebrate`'s 4th argument `quiet`). Team lifts assume near-linear rate against width. Find a board's rate table with `trackOf(b)` (it returns `RATES[b]`). Churn is not a board: `churn = CHURN0*(1+FX.churn)`, set in `refx`.
- **Customers keep their sign-up price.** `subs` maps `"price:plan"` keys (`key`, `unkey`) to customer counts, and each group churns at its own price times `PLANS[plan].stick`. Add customers with `addSub` and change plan with `moveSub(k, to, n=1)`, the one place customers change plan. `unkey` clamps the plan to 0..2. Change `paying` and `subs` together, and use `MRR()` (price × plan multiple) rather than `paying*price`. `load` rewrites old bare price keys as Base.
- **The board scales to fit the screen from 860px up.** CSS clamps the canvas to 176-400px wide from the viewport height, so all six boards fit down to about 750px tall. Below 860px it stays full width. It only changes display size: physics stays at 360x1224 (`360/1224` in the aspect ratio and the fit `clamp`), so tuned widths are unaffected. Canvas text uses `mono()`, which grows by `textK` (set in `resize`, capped at 1.85) as the board shrinks. Header text passes `fillText`'s maxWidth so it condenses instead of overflowing.
- **Expansion (board 5) is locked until `EXP_AT` ($1M peak valuation, `expOpen()`).** It's drawn greyed out with an unlock label until then. Nothing flows into it from Revenue: `land` ends paying users at board 4. Its widths come from `sim/expansion.js` (uniform entry): `EXP_W0` centre Upgrade slot (about 12%, `EXP_RATE0`) and two `DOWN_ZONES` of `DOWN_W` 12px in from the walls (about 3.8%, `DOWN_RATE`), which also act as walls in `physics`. Slots flush with the walls don't work: at 4px or less nothing lands, and at 5px already 6%. `BOARDS[5].bench` is null, so the header shows only "yours". `into`/`out` have `NB` entries; `load` pads old 5-entry saves with 0.
- **Expansion flow.** Once a game month (`expCheck` in the rollover branch of `loop`), if `expOpen()`, each `subs` group checks `m = binom(n, EXP_CHECK)` customers (1/12). Up to the free ball slots (`ballCap - balls.length`) go through `spawn(enter(5,0))`, at random x, with `o.sub` set to the group key. The rest settle in bulk with the odds path's rates: `up = binom(rest, rateOf(trackOf(5)))`, `down = binom(rest - up, DOWN_RATE/(1 - rate))`, ignoring up for Enterprise and down for Base, applied by `moveSub(k, to, n)`; `into[5]` gains all `m`. No per-customer loop, so keep it that way. `subs` is snapshotted first because landing moves customers between groups. `landExp`: centre slot moves the customer up one plan, a `DOWN_ZONES` slot down one, both through `moveSub`; anything else, Enterprise up, Base down, or a group that emptied meanwhile counts as Same plan. `out[5]` counts upgrades that happened. The odds path in `spawn` places upgrades at `rateOf(trackOf(5))`, downgrades at `DOWN_RATE` and misses outside both slot kinds. There is deliberately no downgrade effect key: `DOWN_ZONES` are physics walls of fixed width, so an odds-only effect would make outcomes depend on the ball cap. A ball that enters the Upgrade slot and can upgrade gets `o.up` and is drawn in `C.exp`. Expansion-wedge nodes (`exp1` and anything with area 5) are `closed` before `expOpen()`; the account manager (`am`) widens the Upgrade slot like other roles.
- **One game month is 10 real seconds** (`MONTH`). Monthly rates are scaled by `dt/MONTH`.
- **Net revenue retention is a chained monthly approximation, not cohort tracking.** `flow={start,exp,con,lost,new}` holds this month's MRR movement: `start` is MRR at month start, `moveSub` adds plan price differences to `exp`/`con`, churn and `loseCustomers` add to `lost` (from every customer, including this month's sign-ups), and `new` is the MRR of customers who joined this month (Revenue landings, Rival-shut-down event; never `moveSub`). At rollover the ledger entry gets `nrr=max(0,(start+exp-con-lost*start/(start+new))/start)`, so losses are attributed to the start cohort by exposure; null if start is 0. Then `flow` resets with `start=MRR()`. `NRR12()` multiplies the last 12 non-null ratios, else null (a fresh game's first month is null, so first value comes at about month 13). Old saves lack `flow` (load sets `start=MRR()`) and `nrr`. The Upgrades stage and the Company panel row both show a 109% benchmark, the median net dollar retention in KeyBanc's 2022 private SaaS survey (via Kellblog), cited in `.src`.

## Balance

`tests/bot.js` is a balance script, not a test. It plays a greedy policy headless and prints a line every 12 game months (MRR, valuation, cash, 3-month margin, NRR, year-on-year growth, Rule of 40, team with CSMs, MMs and engineers, roadmap points `rp`/cap and income, nodes owned (repeats counted), `stale`, the IPO hold, board confidence (`ULT` while an ultimatum runs, `–` before the board), any open case with its due month, purchases) and a final line with the months to $1M/$10M/$100M/$1B, the IPO filing, the ending and its month (for `idle`, also months after $10M) or `no ending`, the lowest board confidence, months under 40, when the bridge was taken, cases opened with their month and ruling band (`closed`, `order`, `shut` or `open`), the longest run of months without a node after $1M, good and bad event counts (bad ones by name) and the final hidden alignment.

```sh
node tests/bot.js                      # 600 game months, full policy (cheapest node), 50 ms frames
node tests/bot.js 600 growth           # prefers Acquisition and Referral nodes
node tests/bot.js 600 retention        # prefers Activation, Retention and any churn cut
node tests/bot.js 600 enterprise       # prefers Revenue and Expansion nodes
node tests/bot.js 600 idle             # full until $10M peak, then buys nothing; the board should oust it, never file
node tests/bot.js 600 harsh            # full, but fork options with al[0] < 0 at 55% of their price; draws cases
node tests/bot.js 600 nomargin         # ads first, no margin check, up to 3 campaign renewals per decision
node tests/bot.js early                # months 6 and 12 of a social-ads-only start
```

The policies share one rule set. Roadmap nodes: `full` takes the cheapest available node it has the points for. The build policies weigh each node's cash price by preference (a fork option in their areas at 55%, another node in their areas at 75%, the rest in full; keystones count when either side is theirs) and work toward the lowest, waiting for points unless they are at the cap; ties break at random, so fork sides vary between runs. Before $1M peak, nodes compete with SEO tiers and channel unlocks under the old cash rule (buy the cheapest affordable one, saving for it when 6 months of gross profit would cover it). From $1M, nodes come out of a roadmap budget of `BOT_RD` (15%) of revenue, kept for up to `BOT_KEEP` (24) months, and only nodes that budget could ever cover are targets; without the budget the bot poured all cash into cheap repeatables and starved hiring and ads. Engineers are hired while points are short (below 3/4 of the cap, or an available node needs more than the cap) and net cash flow after the hire still covers the next node's price within 6 months. The rest is unchanged: each of `ROLES` at 3; CSMs to cover customers (Cover all, else +10s, else singles); MMs while net cash flow stays positive after the hire, +10 at a time where possible; ads on every open channel down to half reach, plus one new campaign, while `margin3()` is above 10%; never borrow, except that every policy takes the emergency bridge when offered (`bridgeOffer()`) while `netFlow()` is 0 or less or cash is negative; once `stale` passes `STALE_GRACE`, ship the cheapest node it can buy now (`canBuy`), budget or not, as a player answering the board's Nothing shipped would (not in `idle` after it stops); post when ready; file as soon as it can. The bot stops at an ending (`over`). `harsh` is `full` with fork options whose `al[0]` is below 0 weighed at 55% of their cash price. Net cash flow uses `cts()`. The bot steps frames at 50 ms (the game uses about 16 ms; `loop` caps dt at 50 ms), overrides `setTimeout` so referred friends arrive, and stubs `draw` and most of `hud` for speed. Unseeded, so run several: a run takes about 35 s to 2.5 min of CPU.

Constants after the roadmap balance (November 2026): `NODE_CASH` [0, 2k, 4k, 8k, 14k, 20k, 25k] × `mkt()^AD_PRICE_P` (.6), `EXP_CASH` 2.5, `exp1` $5,000; points = ring (keystones 6, repeatables 4 + times bought); `RP_FOUNDER` .5, `RP_ENG` .5 (`ENG_K` 5, `ENG_P` .5), `RP_PD` .15, `RP_CAP_MONTHS` 3, `RP_CAP_MIN` 6, `RP_MILE` [2,3,5,8,12], `RP_START` 2; `STALE_GRACE` 6, `STALE_STEP` .02, `STALE_MAX` .3; relative effects multiply, floored at `FX_FLOOR` .1; rate bonuses soft-capped from `RATE_KNEE` .7; `ALIGN_SCALE` 5, `EVENT_AL_K` 2.

Results from November 2026 with those constants, rerun after `rateOf` started reading the clamped slot width (the earlier numbers had odds-path rates above the physical slot, some above 100%) and the falling-behind penalty started switching off: 12 runs of each build policy (two batches of 6) and 2 idle, 600 months each. Nothing was retuned.
- Filing months, sorted: full 281, 301, 340, 352, 361, 381, 386, 406, 407, 407, 447, 471 (median 384); growth 342, 355, 363, 381, 404, 427, 437, 448, 463, 551, never, never (median 432); retention 283, 321, 323, 330, 333, 394, 402, 432, 444, 447, 494, never (median 398); enterprise 310, 336, 355, 357, 366, 369, 391, 396, 418, 586, never, never (median 380). Medians are within 14% of each other, which meets the no-dominant-policy target (25%). Before the fix they were 361-403.
- **Not met: filing inside months 360-540 in at least 2 of 3 runs.** In the window: full 8 of 12, growth 7 of 12, retention 6 of 12, enterprise 5 of 12 (26 of 48, against 27 of 48 before the fix). Runs land early (below 360) or, after a slow start, late or never: 4 of the 5 that never filed reached $1M only at months 420-564. $1M arrives anywhere from month 96 to 564, a wider spread than the 180-month window. Narrowing it needs early-game changes (seed price, early saving, churn at tiny customer counts), not tree constants. The fifth miss (growth) reached $1M at 96 and $1B at 447 but overgrew its margin, like `nomargin`: Rule of 40 below 40 in 71 of 156 months at $100M+ ARR, NRR 97% at month 600.
- A node at least every 60 months after $1M: met by all 48 build runs (longest gaps 11-60 months).
- Idle never filed: both stalled at $10M-plus, with 360-376 months without a node. Nomargin was not rerun (before the fix it filed at 275 and 309).
- Bad events: 26-71 a run. Builds whose good/bad exposure ended at -.15 or below drew 0-20 alignment events (Regulator fine, Class action, Data breach, Viral complaint), up to 54% of their bad news, and most still filed (fastest runs included). Near-neutral builds drew 0-6.
- NRR: in the yearly lines, NRR first reads 110% or more at $0.8M-$33M MRR in 45 of 48 build runs; the other three are two slow starts that never got far and the growth run above. It climbs once CSMs, Expansion and account managers stack.
- At filing, runs had made 37-47 node purchases (a repeatable counts once per purchase), against 92 distinct nodes besides the start node, so more than half the tree is still unbought late.

Game-over balance (November 2026). The spec's starting values made nearly every run end: 8 of the first 10 `full` runs lost, mostly in the first 6 years. What changed, and why:
- Board reason sizes: Nothing shipped capped at −3 instead of −5 and Shrinking −2 instead of −3 (slow-growing runs that shipped were ousted from yoy noise); Growing from 40% to 20% `yoy()`; Shipped +2 → +3.
- Rules: `softLocked()` needs `!canPost()` (it ended runs that later posted their way to the IPO); `bridgeSize()` clears the overdraft; Shut down needs an earlier order on the axis (`lawStrikes`; greedy builds reach −.75 to −1 users exposure, so a single ruling shut most `harsh` runs). The bot ships when `stale` passes `STALE_GRACE` and takes the bridge only while burning or overdrawn (before that, its roadmap budget let `stale` reach 13-19 months and most ousters were its own).
- Insolvency and the bridge only count from `RISK_AT` ($100k peak), so the early game's slow starts stay overdrawn instead of ending (before that, 7 of 20 `full` runs ran out of runway in months 15-79 with 1-5 customers).
- Constants, with `RISK_AT` in place: 20 `full` runs each, changing one value from the final set at a time. All spec values (`INSOLVENT_MONTHS` 3, `CASE_FINE` 6, `BRIDGE_MIN` $5,000): 7 of 20 ended (5 out of runway, 2 of them right after a consent order; 2 ousted). Final set (6, 3, $5,000): 2 of 20. `INSOLVENT_MONTHS` 3 alone: 3 of 20, 2 out of runway right after an order. `CASE_FINE` 6 alone: 2 of 20, both out of runway right after an order. `BRIDGE_MIN` $1,000: 3 of 20. So `BRIDGE_MIN` is back at $5,000, and `INSOLVENT_MONTHS` 6 and `CASE_FINE` 3 stay: with either spec value an order leaves a company overdrawn for longer than the count allows, so the order band ends runs like Shut down. `SOFTLOCK_MONTHS` is 2 as specced.

Results with the final constants (`RISK_AT` $100k, `INSOLVENT_MONTHS` 6, `SOFTLOCK_MONTHS` 2, `BRIDGE_MIN` $5,000, `CASE_FINE` 3, the board sizes in Gotchas), 600 months each:
- Full: 2 of 20 ended (out of runway at month 97 before $1M, ousted at 207). Met: at most 1 in 10. Filing months: 295, 296, 310, 318, 346, 358, 369, 372, 373, 374, 379, 383, 384, 405, 405, 418, 423, 459 (median 374, against 384 before game-overs). 6 runs had cases: 6 consent orders, 1 closed, 1 still open at filing.

The other 2 ran out of runway at 52 and 95, before $1M. Shut down now always follows an order, but a build that keeps pushing exposure down still ends there.
